# Phase 2 benchmark (2026-10-14)

highspy 1.15.1 (HiGHS 1.15.1, revision 04024d7), Python 3.12.7, 18 worker processes on 24 threads, next to other work. All times are CPU seconds of one process (user plus system) in native HiGHS with the project's determinism options (presolve off), unless a line says otherwise. Wall time on this shared machine was 1.15x the CPU time. CPU hours are hours of one thread while every other thread is busy too. Catalog 2026-10-14: 2,192 wiki-level wearable items.

## Result

- **Precompute scope.** Tiers +0, +4 and +10 are precomputed and the other tiers solve in the browser: the full grid needs 681 CPU hours with the tie stage and the wiki-first cut, above the 300 hour budget; tiers 0/4/10 alone need 189.
- **What decides it.** The tie stage is most of the cost; the first solve alone would need 295 CPU hours for all tiers under the same cuts, within the 300 hour budget.
- **Presolve.** The gate passes on 500 scenarios (0 mismatches), but presolve runs at 0.71x the speed of presolve off on this formulation, so it stays off.
- **Phone budget** (median under 1 s, p95 under 5 s). highs-js on the desktop: median 107 ms, p95 949 ms; at 4x slower (a mid-range phone) 429 and 3,796 ms: within the budget.

## Presolve gate

500 scenarios drawn uniformly from the grid (all tiers, races, trios, roles and setups, both buff sets, wiki and strict-proxy pools), each solved with presolve off and with presolve on. **Objective mismatches: 0** (a difference above 1e-09 relative; the largest seen is 0.0e+00). Presolve-on runs that did not end optimal (to be re-solved with presolve off): 0. Errors: 0. Speed-up of presolve (time off over time on; below 1 is slower): solve 0.71x, with the tie stage 0.49x.

On the 100 scenarios that also ran the canonical solve: 0 objective mismatches; the same items were worn in 100 and the same kit (every item in the same place) in 85. Equal value with an item in another place is a tie that the canonical stage does not break (see the notes).

## Notes

- Strict level: the public catalog has no strict items, so a strict solve is stood in for by a fixed 44% of the wiki items. Against its wiki twin it costs 0.46x to prepare, 0.45x to solve and 0.43x with the tie stage. The 74 percent of strict solves that wiki-first skips was measured on the previous model; it cannot be measured without strict items.
- Sampling: the coverage sample has three races and tiers 0, 4 and 10; the other eight tiers are interpolated. The uniform sample (all 15 races, all 11 tiers) is the check. The interval covers the choice of trios only, and treats a stratum with a single sampled trio as exact.
- Where the time goes (mean CPU seconds per production scenario, 1.57): preparing the model 0.20 (13%), the first solve 0.48 (30%), the tie stage 0.89 (57%).
- The trios with three mana pools that mix WIS and INT (selection binaries) are 30% of the grid and 69% of its time with the tie stage: 3.22 CPU seconds a scenario against 0.60.
- Ties: between presolve off and on, the same items were worn in 499 of 500 plain solves and 100 of 100 solves with the tie stage, but every item in the same place in only 381 and 85. An item that fits a slot and the Any slots can swap places with equal value and equal rank sum (a chest piece in the chest or in an Any slot), so the tie stage does not fix the places; the kits agree up to that.
- Clock: times are the CPU time of the process, whose tick is 1/64 s; one short step reads coarsely, and sums over thousands of scenarios do not. Other work shared the machine while the workers ran.
- Not measured: the build-once cut. Preparing a model is 13% of a production scenario (preparation plus the solve and tie stage), which is the most that cut could save.
- The robustness seal (about 55,000 extra solves) would add about 24 CPU hours at the mean cost of a production scenario.
